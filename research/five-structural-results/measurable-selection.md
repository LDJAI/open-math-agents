# A measurable map that cannot be lifted through a continuous surjection

## Question and result

For a standard Borel space $S$, let $\mathcal A(S)$ be the $\sigma$-algebra generated by its analytic subsets. Call a map analytically measurable when inverse images of Borel sets belong to $\mathcal A(S)$. A set is universally measurable if it lies in the completion of every Borel probability measure. These are different measurability conditions.

On July 15, 2013, the author SBF asked whether the following lifting assertion holds. Let $X,Y,Z$ be standard Borel spaces, $K\subseteq X\times Y$ analytic, $g:X\times Y\to Z$ Borel and $h:X\to Z$ analytically measurable. Suppose $h(x)\in g(x,K_x)$ everywhere. Must some analytically measurable $f:X\to Y$ satisfy $(x,f(x))\in K$ and $g(x,f(x))=h(x)$ everywhere? The original post has no posted answer at the time checked. George Lowther predicted a negative answer to this stated question. SBF also considered the weaker universally measurable conclusion and wrote that it was “unlikely it does contain a graph of a universally measurable map.” SBF's own profile reports a PhD in probability and control. [Original post and comments](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/136792/existence-of-an-universally-measurable-pullback) · [SBF's profile](https://mathoverflow.net/users/11768/sbf)

**Theorem 1.** The analytically measurable conclusion fails, even when $K=X\times Y$, the spaces are Polish, and $g(x,y)=p(y)$ for a fixed continuous surjection $p:Y\to Z$. Under the hypotheses as stated, a universally measurable lift always exists. It can be chosen as the composition of two analytically measurable maps.

**Theorem 2.** For a Polish space $Y$, the following property holds if and only if $Y$ is $\sigma$-compact. For every standard Borel $X$, every Polish $Z$, every continuous $p:Y\to Z$, and every analytically measurable $h:X\to Z$ with $h(X)\subseteq p(Y)$, there exists an analytically measurable $f:X\to Y$ with $p\circ f=h$.

## 1. A diagonal obstruction

Take the Cantor space $W=2^{\mathbb N}$. A Borel recipe for a subset of $W$ is a well-founded, countably branching, rooted tree whose leaves are labeled by basic clopen cylinders, with unary complement and countable union at its internal nodes. These recipes represent all Borel sets. Fix a Polish space $B$ of arbitrary candidate codes: for example, use countable sequences recording the presence and labels of nodes of $\mathbb N^{<\mathbb N}$, with entries in a countable discrete alphabet. A root, prefix closure, allowed labels and arities are syntactic Borel requirements. Infinite branches of a candidate tree are witnessed by elements of Baire space $\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}$. The set of invalid codes (failure of syntax or existence of a branch) is therefore analytic. The ambient space $B$ includes all codes, including the invalid ones.

For $e\in\{0,1\}$, write $E_e(b,w)$ when there exists a Boolean labeling of the nodes that is consistent with the syntax of $b$ at $w$ and gives the root value $e$. Consistency is a Borel relation in $b,w$ and the labeling: a leaf tests cylinder membership, a complement reverses its child's bit, and a union has value one precisely when at least one child has value one. These are countably many Borel conditions. Thus $E_e$ is analytic. On a valid well-founded code, recursion on tree rank gives precisely one value on each node. In particular exactly one of $E_0(b,w),E_1(b,w)$ holds and the root bit is the indicator of the coded Borel set, denoted $B_b$.

Let $T=\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}$ and put $X=B\times T^{\mathbb N}$. This Polish space is fixed before choosing a universal analytic set $U\subseteq T\times X$ whose sections $U_a$ range over all analytic subsets of $X$. Such a $U$ is obtained by coding closed subsets of $X\times\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}$ and projecting out the last coordinate. In the concrete zero-dimensional spaces chosen here, trees of finite coordinate approximations supply the closed-set codes.

For $x=(b,(a_j)_{j\ge0})$, define
$$
 h(x)_j=\mathbf1_U(a_j,x).
$$
The map $x\mapsto(a_j,x)$ is Borel for each $j$, so each coordinate-one event is analytic. Cylinders generate the Borel sets of $W$, hence $h:X\to W$ is analytically measurable.

Take $Y_0=W\times\{0,1\}$ with output map $g_0(x,(w,i))=w$, and impose the analytic constraint
$$
 K_0=\{(x,(w,i)):\ b(x)\text{ is invalid or } E_{1-i}(b(x),w)\}.
$$
Every $(x,w)$ has an admissible bit $i$. For valid $b(x)$ this bit is forced to equal $1-\mathbf1_{B_{b(x)}}(w)$.

Suppose there were an analytically measurable admissible lift $f_0(x)=(h(x),i(x))$. Its bit-one set $A=\{x:i(x)=1\}$ would belong to $\mathcal A(X)$. Every such $A$ can be written
$$
 \mathbf1_A(t)=\mathbf1_{B_b}\big((\mathbf1_{U_{a_j}}(t))_{j\ge0}\big)
 \quad\text{for all }t\in X                                   \tag{1}
$$
with a valid Borel recipe $b$ and fixed analytic-set codes $a_j$. Indeed, the family of sets obtainable by testing a Borel condition on countably many analytic membership predicates contains all analytic sets and is closed under complements and countable unions: combine countably many lists into one list and use the corresponding coordinate projections. Conversely, every such test belongs to $\mathcal A(X)$.

Choose $x_*=(b,(a_j)_j)$ from (1). At $t=x_*$, (1) gives $i(x_*)=\mathbf1_{B_b}(h(x_*))$. Admissibility forces $i(x_*)=1-\mathbf1_{B_b}(h(x_*))$. This contradiction rules out every analytically measurable lift.

## 2. Removing the constraint and obtaining a fixed continuous surjection

The analytic set $K_0$ is the projection of a closed set
$$
 C\subseteq X\times W\times\{0,1\}\times\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}.
$$
Give $C$ the subspace topology. It is Polish. The projection $p:C\to X\times W$, $(u,w,i,r)\mapsto(u,w)$, is continuous and onto, because $K_0$ has an admissible bit for every pair $(u,w)$. Define $H(x)=(x,h(x))$. This is analytically measurable by testing product cylinders. An analytically measurable lift of $H$ through $p$ would, by the continuous coordinate projection $(u,w,i,r)\mapsto(w,i)$, produce the impossible lift of Section 1. Taking $Y=C$, $Z=X\times W$, $K=X\times C$ and $g(x,c)=p(c)$ proves the negative part of Theorem 1 in its strong form.

## 3. Universal measurability

For the general input of the question, put
$$
 R=\{((x,z),y):(x,y)\in K,\ g(x,y)=z\}.
$$
This is analytic. Its projection $D\subseteq X\times Z$ is analytic and contains every pair $(x,h(x))$. The Jankov–von Neumann uniformization theorem gives a selector $s:D\to Y$ measurable for the restriction of $\mathcal A(X\times Z)$. It can be extended outside $D$ by a fixed value in $Y$: relative analytic subsets of $D$ are traces of analytic sets, and $D$ itself is analytic. The empty cases are immediate. The two maps $x\mapsto(x,h(x))$ and $s$ are analytically measurable and therefore universally measurable. The map
$$
 f(x)=s(x,h(x))
$$
is universally measurable and satisfies both requirements everywhere.

For the composition step, fix a Borel probability measure $\mu$ on the source and let $k$ be a universally measurable map. On the $\mu$-completion it has a Borel probability pushforward $\nu$ on the target. Any universally measurable target set agrees up to a subset of a $\nu$-null Borel set with a Borel target set. Pulling back shows that its inverse image under $k$ is in the $\mu$-completion. This works for every $\mu$. Universal composition and the failure of closure of analytic measurability under composition are classical. A bad composition, by itself, does not preclude a different analytically measurable selector for the same lifting problem, which accounts for the explicit diagonal argument. [Bertsekas and Shreve, 1978, Chapter 7, Proposition 7.44 and Section 7.7](https://web.mit.edu/dimitrib/www/SOC_1978.pdf)

## 4. Characterization by the lifting space

Suppose first that $Y=\bigcup_nK_n$ with each $K_n$ compact. For a continuous $p:Y\to Z$, the images $p(K_n)$ are compact. Each restriction $p:K_n\to p(K_n)$ has a Borel right inverse: the inverse graph is closed with nonempty compact sections, so compact-section Borel uniformization applies. On $p(Y)$ choose the first $n$ for which $z\in p(K_n)$ and use that right inverse. This gives a Borel right inverse on the Borel set $p(Y)$. Composing with $h$ gives the desired analytically measurable lifting. The countable-compact direction is a classical Filippov lifting theorem, proved in this form for more general measurable sources by McShane and Warfield. [McShane and Warfield, 1967, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1967-0208590-X) · [Himmelberg and Van Vleck, 1969](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1969-041-7)

Conversely, suppose $Y$ is Polish and is not $\sigma$-compact. Hurewicz's theorem gives a closed subspace $F$ of $Y$ homeomorphic to Baire space. Since every nonempty Polish space is a continuous image of Baire space, choose a continuous surjection $r:F\to C$ onto the witness space of Section 2. Let $p_0:C\to Z_0=X\times W$ be the continuous surjection there and let $H:X\to Z_0$ be the map with no analytically measurable lift. Choose a topological embedding $e:Z_0\to[0,1]^{\mathbb N}$. Each coordinate of $e\circ p_0\circ r$ on $F$ extends to a continuous $[0,1]$-valued map on $Y$ by Tietze's theorem. Combine these extensions into $J:Y\to[0,1]^{\mathbb N}$. For a compatible metric $d$ on $Y$, define
$$
 p_1(y)=(J(y),\min(1,d(y,F))),\qquad
 H_1(x)=(e(H(x)),0).
$$
The map $p_1$ is continuous and $H_1$ is analytically measurable. Its image lies in $p_1(Y)$ since $p_0$ and $r$ are surjective. A lift $v$ with $p_1\circ v=H_1$ must take every value in the closed set $F$. If it were analytically measurable, so would be its corestriction to $F$ and then $r\circ v$. The injectivity of $e$ makes $r\circ v$ an analytically measurable lift of $H$ through $p_0$, a contradiction. This proves Theorem 2. [Kechris, *Classical Descriptive Set Theory*, Theorem 7.10](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4) · [Statement of the Hurewicz dichotomy](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/home/luecke/publications/Hurewicz.pdf)

## Scope and prior work

The formal three-condition assertion receives the negative answer Lowther expected. The universal version in the title and SBF's comment receives an affirmative answer, as a consequence of established selection and composition theorems. The example makes the exact analytic-measurability obstruction visible even for an unconstrained continuous surjection. The classification in Theorem 2 concerns fixed maps $p(y)$ continuous in the action variable and the range-containment hypothesis; the original problem also allowed a Borel map depending on the state and an arbitrary analytic constraint. In dynamic programming, this distinction affects whether a state-dependent choice can be represented in the requested measurability class. The construction gives no algorithm for finding policies.

Earlier work explicitly distinguishes analytic, universal, and larger composition-stable classes in selection and stochastic optimization. Shreve's 1978 article treats a related exact-optimization selection problem and a composition-stable hierarchy. Single-objective exact minimization and game equilibrium have additional structure and are separate questions. A search located antecedents for the positive results, the complement-class hierarchy and the classical uniformization tools. It did not establish priority for the explicit negative lifting assertion or the exact characterization stated here.
