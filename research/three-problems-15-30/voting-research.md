# Computing pairwise marginals of a distribution on rankings

[Left To Solve mo-27245](https://lefttosolve.com/p/mo-27245) imports [David R. MacIver's June 6, 2010 question](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/27245/distribution-on-permutations-derived-from-probability-of-pairwise-orderings). The question asks how to compute the probability that one item precedes another in the distribution obtained by conditioning independent pairwise choices on transitivity. The author would accept an efficient approximation. Suresh Venkatasubramanian's 2010 answer conjectured counting hardness, and raised the possibility of approximation. Counting hardness alone does not rule out approximation.

## Conditional worst-case lower bound

Consider rational inputs with all off-diagonal probabilities in [1/3, 2/3], satisfying reciprocity. A deterministic polynomial-time procedure that always returns the requested single pairwise marginal to additive error 1/4 would imply P = NP. The assumption concerns arbitrary inputs in that interval and a worst-case guarantee. Structured ranking models or typical instances may permit fast numerical estimates. The construction uses a polynomial-time Turing reduction. Its large degree makes it unsuitable as a practical benchmarking instance generator.

Given a tournament with n ≥ 2 candidates and m = n(n−1)/2 arcs, let S(σ) count arcs pointing forward in ranking σ. Finding a maximizer of S solves the minimum feedback arc set problem for tournaments, which [Charbit, Thomassé and Yeo proved NP-hard](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548306007887).

Process the m comparisons in a fixed order. Maintain the invariant that some maximizer of S realizes all previously chosen directions. For k fixed decisions let
V(σ) = S(σ) + (number of past decisions respected by σ).
The maximizers of V are exactly the maximizers of S respecting all k decisions. Indeed, an optimum of S respecting k decisions exists by induction, and no ranking exceeds it in either summand. Tying the sum therefore requires tying both summands.

Write V as a sum of two possible nonnegative integer payoffs per pair, one for each orientation. Each orientation receives zero or one for the tournament arc and zero or one for the stored decision. The two payoffs differ by an integer d in {−2,−1,0,1,2}. Set q=4/3 and take raw odds q^d, so p=q^d/(1+q^d). Every comparison probability belongs to the fixed set {9/25, 3/7, 1/2, 4/7, 16/25}. All are strictly between 1/3 and 2/3, so every directed triangle inequality p_ik ≥ p_ij+p_jk−1 holds automatically.

Replace each candidate by a block of L=256n² labeled clones. Within a block the odds are one. All pairs across two blocks inherit their blocks' raw odds. A clone ranking τ has weight proportional to q^C(τ), where C sums the corresponding orientation payoffs over the cross-block pairs. Every normalization denominator depends only on the pair, so it cancels between complete rankings.

Choose independently one uniform representative in each block. For a fixed τ the induced original ranking σ has E[V(σ) | τ]=C(τ)/L² by summing the marginal payoffs over ordered pairs of representatives. Consequently C(τ) ≤ L² max V. Ordering each entire block contiguously according to an optimal original ranking attains equality.

Draw τ from its Gibbs distribution. If Z is its partition function, the entropy identity H(τ)=log Z−E[C] log q and the existence of a single best ranking give (max C−E[C]) log q ≤ H(τ) ≤ log((nL)!). Thus the expected regret of the induced original ranking is bounded by n log(nL)/(L log q). Since log(4/3) ≥ 1/4 and log(nL) = log 256 + 3 log n < 6 + 3n, the bound is less than (6+3n)/(64n) ≤ 3/32 < 1/8 for n≥2. Regret is a nonnegative integer, so the probability that the representatives fail to maximize V is below 1/8.

For the next original comparison, query all L² cross-block clone pairs and average the estimates. The average of their true probabilities is exactly the comparison probability for the random representatives. The triangle inequality bounds the error of the average by 1/4. Equivalently, one can use a single pair of designated clones per query: within-block relabeling preserves the Gibbs weights and the ranking space, so every one of the L² marginal probabilities is equal. Choose the direction whose estimated probability is at least 1/2, resolving an exact tie arbitrarily. Additive error 1/4 ensures this direction has true probability at least 1/4. Its event therefore contains a V-maximizer, proving the invariant at the next step. After m steps every pair has been decided, specifying an optimal original ranking. The averaging implementation uses mL² oracle calls, polynomial in n. Each input probability comes from the five-element set above. The number of clones and the input bit length are polynomial in n.

## Exact inference at moderate input probabilities

Unrestricted exact marginal #P-hardness was proved in 2008 by [Chen, Fortnow, Lambert, Pennock and Wortman, equation (9) and Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1362), for the identical Gibbs distribution in a logarithmic-market-scoring-rule pair-betting market. Their proof uses odds as large as n!. Their approximate pricing algorithm concerns subset bets and a market-maker loss bound. The following interpolation argument keeps all input probabilities in [1/3,2/3]. Take a finite poset, and list its m ordered covering comparisons. For x in [1/2,1], each respected comparison contributes weight 1 and each violated one contributes x. All other comparisons are neutral. Let Z(x) be the sum of permutation weights. Its constant coefficient is the number of linear extensions, and its degree is at most m. Construct a chain of models adding the constraints one at a time. The ratio of successive partition functions is a+(1−a)x, where a is the previous model's marginal probability of respecting the added comparison. Z starts at n!, so an exact marginal oracle evaluates Z(x) with m calls. Interpolation at m+1 distinct rational inputs recovers its constant coefficient. For example use x=(m+j)/(2m) for j=0,…,m when m>0. All numerators and denominators involved have polynomial bit length. Counting linear extensions is #P-complete by [Brightwell and Winkler](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00383444). The argument gives #P-hardness of exact pairwise marginals under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

## Computing the requested marginal

For a practical exact calculation on small inputs, let Z(S) be the sum of all permutation weights on subset S. Group by the last candidate j. Then Z(∅)=1 and Z(S)=Σ_{j∈S} Z(S∖{j}) ∏_{i∈S∖{j}} P_ij. Use the same recurrence with P_ba replaced by zero to retain only permutations in which a precedes b. The desired marginal is the ratio of the modified full-set sum to the original one. The auxiliary zero enforces an event and need not preserve reciprocity. Subset products can be precomputed, giving O(n 2ⁿ) arithmetic operations and O(n 2ⁿ) space per requested pair. This decomposition provides an exact computation; it need not be new.

## Prior art and limits

The ranking distribution predates the 2010 question. It is the Babington Smith model from 1950. [Joe and Verducci (1993), Theorems 3.2.1 and 3.3.1](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/32486/1/Michael%20A.%20Fligner_1993.pdf) relate its raw parameters and marginal probabilities. Theorem 3.2.1 gives the triangle inequalities from the question. The triangle inequalities do not force the raw probabilities to equal the marginals. For a cyclic three-item input with clockwise probability p, three orders have two clockwise edges and three have one. The expected number of clockwise edges is 1+p, so any given clockwise edge has marginal (1+p)/3. At p=3/5 every triangle constraint holds but the true marginal is 8/15. At p=9/10 the marginal is 19/30, correcting the 1/2 in the question. Computation under related probability models and estimation objectives already appears in [Azari Soufiani, Parkes and Xia (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.7856). We do not claim that the finite example or the underlying probability model is new. We have not located these exact bounded-input reductions in the sources reviewed. A comprehensive novelty determination is still open.

The 2010 question does not impose a precision, define efficiency, or fix a family of practical inputs. The two reductions give precise worst-case answers. Lean 4.33.1 certifies the finite Gibbs entropy bound, the clone averaging and optimum identities over injective ranking positions, the explicit constant bound log((nL)!)/(L² log(4/3)) < 1/8 at L=256n², the quarter-error oracle selection bound, the integer-regret criterion for an optimal witness, and the preservation of optimality by a unit payoff bonus. Each checked theorem used only the kernel-approved standard axioms. The files separate these ingredients. The complete encoding of the oracle computation, its polynomial time and size bounds as algorithms, and the #P interpolation proof have not been verified end to end in Lean. No official solution target was available on the site at the time of inspection. The unbounded-odds test covers every four-item tournament and every admissible oracle decision. A separate exact two-clone test exhausts all 720 clone rankings for each of 32 three-item weighted models and verifies representative-score averaging, the exact optimum, and within-block exchangeability.
